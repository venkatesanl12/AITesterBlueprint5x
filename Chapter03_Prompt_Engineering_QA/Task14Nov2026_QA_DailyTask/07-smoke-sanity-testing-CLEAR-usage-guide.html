Smoke and Sanity Testing — CLEAR reference guide

Template file07-smoke-sanity-testing-CLEAR.md
FrameworkCLEAR
TaskTime-boxed go/no-go verdict
Guide07 of 17

What it does. Produces a time-boxed checklist of binary checks — broad smoke on a new build, or narrow sanity on a specific fix — with a GO / NO-GO verdict and classified blocking issues.

Use when

A build has landed and someone needs a readiness answer now: is this worth testing further, or is it going back?

Skip when

You need full functional confirmation — use 04-test-case-execution-CLEAR.md or 09-regression-testing-STAR.md. Smoke is breadth over depth by design.

1

Read the framework before you paste anything

CLEAR is the right shape here because the value is in the fence: a fixed time budget, binary checks, and a verdict that must follow from the results.

C
ContextWhat is being run, on which build, with which evidence.
L
LimitsThe fence: allowed statuses, allowed scope, what may not be invented.
E
ExamplesSample rows showing the exact status and evidence shape.
A
ActionThe single deliverable you want returned.
R
ReviewThe checks the model must pass before returning the answer.
Rule of thumb. Limits are the whole point of CLEAR — they stop the model awarding an unearned pass. Only C — Context changes between runs; never relax L to get a longer answer.
2

Prepare the inputs first

Two numbers decide whether this works: the time budget and what actually changed. Without the change summary, sanity scope cannot be bounded.

  • Build and environment — build ID, URL, browser, OS.
  • Time budget — in minutes. This is a constraint, not a target.
  • Change summary — required for sanity scope; the changed area plus its dependents only.
  • Critical paths — the short list of journeys that must work for the product to be usable.
  • Smoke or sanity — state which. They have different scope rules and different verdict meanings.

Most common mistake. Saying “test the login area” and expecting a defensible verdict. Without the change summary the model either widens into regression or narrows arbitrarily — both make the verdict meaningless.
4

Audit the output against the contract

Five sections. The verdict must be derivable from the checklist — if a reader cannot reproduce the verdict from the rows, the run failed.

SectionRequiredShape
Scope StatementYesSmoke or Sanity + what is in and out
ChecklistYesID | Area | Check | Binary Expected | Status | Duration
VerdictYesGO / NO-GO / CONDITIONAL + one-line reason
Blocking IssuesYesCheck ID | Observed | Why Blocking | Owner; may say “None”
Time Used vs BudgetYesActual / Budget

Every check has a binary expectation — no “works correctly”. Every failure is classified blocking or non-blocking with a reason. Total duration is compared against the stated budget, explicitly.

6

Run the self-check before you trust the output

Six checks. The scope ones catch the common drift: sanity quietly becoming regression, or smoke quietly becoming exploratory.

  • Every check has a binary expected result — no “works correctly”.
  • Smoke stayed at critical-path breadth; sanity stayed inside the change area.
  • Verdict matches the results with no contradiction.
  • Each failure is labelled blocking or non-blocking with a reason.
  • Total duration is within or explicitly over budget.
  • No exploratory or edge-case checks smuggled in.
Fastest sanity check. Read only the verdict, then read only the checklist. If a reader could reach a different verdict from those rows, the verdict is not earned.
7

Fix the failure, not the whole prompt

SymptomRule breachedCorrection to send
A check says “works correctly”.Binary expectations only.“Rewrite as one observable pass/fail assertion.”
Twelve checks in a fifteen-minute budget.Respect the time budget.“Cut to fit the budget or state the overrun explicitly.”
Verdict is GO with a failed critical check.Verdict derives from results.“Change to NO-GO, or classify that failure as non-blocking with a reason.”
Sanity widened into full regression.Sanity stays in the change area.“Remove checks outside the change area and point at template 09.”
A critical path was untestable and silently dropped.Say so, do not drop.“List it under Blocking Issues as not testable within the budget.”
Exploratory checks appear in the list.No exploratory testing.“Remove them or reclassify as out of scope.”
8

Where the output goes next

Regression testing09-regression-testing-STAR.mdA GO verdict is followed by assurance that nothing else broke.
Test case execution04-test-case-execution-CLEAR.mdFull functional execution once the build is worth the time.
Status report13-status-report-STAR.mdThe verdict becomes a line in stakeholder reporting.

A GO here is permission to spend testing time, not a release approval. Keep the wording precise when you quote it upward — smoke passing is not regression passing.

9

Turn it into a skill

Create skills/smoke-sanity-testing/SKILL.md, paste the frontmatter below, use the template's prompt block as the body, then add the output contract and self-check verbatim.

---
name: smoke-sanity-testing
description: Produce a time-boxed smoke or sanity checklist with binary pass/fail checks, a go/no-go verdict, and classified blocking issues. Use when a new build or hotfix needs a fast readiness decision.
---

Trigger phrases: “smoke test this build”, “sanity check the fix”, “go no-go”, “is this build testable”.

Procedure: confirm smoke vs sanity → bound scope and time → build binary checklist → execute/record → classify failures → issue verdict → self-check.
One framework per file. Do not merge this with another framework's skeleton. Blended sections make the contract ambiguous and break the SKILL.md conversion.
