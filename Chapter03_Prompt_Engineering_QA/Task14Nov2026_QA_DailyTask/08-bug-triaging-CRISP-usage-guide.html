Guide08 of 17

What it does. Turns a pile of defects into a decision table: actionable or not, technical severity, business priority, a decision from a fixed list, the workaround, and the justification a triage meeting will argue over.

Use when

You are preparing or running a triage call and need consistent, defensible calls rather than severity by whoever shouted loudest.

Skip when

You are writing up one defect — use 03-bug-report-CRISP.md. Triage decides what to do; the report explains what happened.

1

Read the framework before you paste anything

The Role is a triage lead who decides severity on impact and priority on timing. Keeping those two independent is the whole discipline of this template.

C
ContextThe situation and raw facts you supply. Per-run editable surface.
R
RoleThe judgement role the model takes on for this document.
I
InstructionsNumbered rules plus the explicit Do NOT list.
S
StyleHow terse, how structured, what vocabulary is banned.
P
ParametersHard limits: allowed scales, missing-data wording, length caps.
Rule of thumb. The Role decides the judgement, the Parameters decide the limit. If the output wavers, tighten the Parameters — not the Role. Only C — Context changes between runs.
2

Prepare the inputs first

Triage needs constraints as much as defects. Without the deadline and the release-blocking scope, priority becomes an opinion.

  • Defect list — IDs, summaries, and enough detail to judge impact.
  • Release deadline or sprint — priority is meaningless without timing.
  • What must be clean to release — login, payments, whatever the gate depends on.
  • Team capacity — developers and days, so “Fix This Sprint” is answerable.
  • Known workarounds — or write none. Do not let the model assume one exists.

Most common mistake. Omitting capacity. Every row then becomes “Fix Now”, which is the same as having no triage at all.
4

Audit the output against the contract

Five sections. The divergence section is the one reviewers read first — it shows where technical impact and business timing disagree.

SectionRequiredShape
Triage TableYesDefect ID | Module | Summary | Severity | Priority | Decision | Business Justification | Workaround | Notes
Needs Info ListYesDefect ID | Missing Information | Who Must Supply
Duplicate MapYesPrimary ID | Duplicate IDs | Reason
Severity/Priority DivergenceYesDefect ID | Severity | Priority | Why They Differ
Triage SummaryYesCounts per decision, count blocking release

Severity: Critical / High / Medium / Low, judged on technical impact only. Priority: P1–P4, judged on business timing only. Decisions limited to Fix Now / Fix This Sprint / Defer / Reject / Duplicate / Needs Info.

What a good run looks like

Filled excerpt from the template — severity rises from impact, priority from release timing, and the divergence row explains the case where they differ.

DEF-101CriticalP1Fix NowMandatory-field enforcement absent; the app entry point is compromised—

DEF-104 is not allowed through as High/P2 by guesswork — the missing information is named and the decision is Needs Info.

6

Run the self-check before you trust the output

Six checks. The severity-versus-priority split and the justification line are what stop triage turning into a priority vote.

  • Severity is technical impact only; priority is business timing only.
  • Every row has a one-line business justification.
  • Every Needs Info row names exactly what is missing and who can supply it.
  • Divergences between severity and priority are explained, not hidden.
  • No defect is deferred without an accepted reason.
  • Decision counts match table rows.
Fastest sanity check. Ask “could this be fixed without the deadline moving?” If yes and it is still P1, severity has leaked into priority.
7

Fix the failure, not the whole prompt

SymptomRule breachedCorrection to send
Priority assigned by gut feel.Business justification required.“Add the one-line business impact, or set Needs Info.”
Severity and priority are the same number in disguise.Two independent judgements.“Re-assess severity on impact, then priority on timing, separately.”
A defect with no reproduction steps is triaged as actionable.Actionability gate.“Set Needs Info and name the missing reproduction steps.”
Divergence exists but is not flagged.Flag every divergence.“Add the divergence row and explain why they differ.”
A workaround is assumed.Never assume a workaround.“Write Not supplied. unless the reporter provided one.”
A decision outside the six values appears.Fixed decision list.“Map it to Fix Now / Fix This Sprint / Defer / Reject / Duplicate / Needs Info.”

9

Turn it into a skill

Create skills/bug-triaging/SKILL.md, paste the frontmatter below, use the template's prompt block as the body, then add the output contract and self-check verbatim.

---
name: bug-triaging
description: Triage a defect list into severity, priority, decision, owner and workaround with business justification, flagging severity/priority divergence, duplicates, and defects that need more information. Use when preparing or running a triage meeting.
---

Trigger phrases: “triage these bugs”, “severity and priority for these defects”, “what do we fix this sprint”, “triage call prep”.

Procedure: validate actionability → assign severity → assign priority → justify → decide → dupes → divergence flags → summary → self-check.
One framework per file. Do not merge this with another framework's skeleton. Blended sections make the contract ambiguous and break the SKILL.md conversion.
