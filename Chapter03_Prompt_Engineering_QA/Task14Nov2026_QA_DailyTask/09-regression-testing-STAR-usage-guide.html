What it does. Builds a tiered regression suite that fits the time you actually have: everything the change touched, every business-critical path, and the historical defect hotspots — with named exclusions, residual risk, entry/exit criteria, and a risk-first execution order.

Use when

A release, sprint, or sizeable change needs a defensible answer to “what do we re-test, and what are we accepting by not re-testing?”

Skip when

You only need a fast readiness verdict — use 07-smoke-sanity-testing-CLEAR.md. Regression is the expensive assurance step, not the gate.

1

Read the framework before you paste anything

STAR suits planning: Situation carries the constraints, Task states the outcome, Action is the tiering procedure, Result is the deliverable set.

S
SituationThe release, change, budget and constraints you supply.
T
TaskThe one outcome required, stated in a sentence.
A
ActionThe ordered analysis steps the model must perform.
R
ResultThe numbered deliverable sections and their shape.
Rule of thumb. STAR is a planning and reporting frame, so the numbers live in S and the contract lives in R. Only S — Situation changes between runs.
2

Prepare the inputs first

Regression scope is decided by three inputs: what changed, what is critical, and how much time you have. Any one missing and the plan is guesswork.

  • Change summary — changelog, fixed defects, new features since the last release.
  • Affected modules — what the change touched, including indirect dependents.
  • Business-critical modules — critical, even if unchanged. These never fall out of scope by default.
  • Time budget — days or tester-hours. The suite must fit it or say why it does not.
  • Historical hotspots — where defects clustered before. If unknown, say not supplied rather than inventing.
  • Environment — URLs, browser/OS matrix, builds.

4

Audit the output against the contract

Seven sections. The suite and the exclusion list carry equal weight — a regression plan that does not say what it excludes is not a scope, it is a wish.

SectionRequiredShape
Scope and Coverage RationaleYesBullets tied to change, criticality, history
Regression SuiteYesTier | Area | Case IDs | Purpose | Time | Data Setup | Priority
Excluded Areas and Residual RiskYesArea | Reason | Risk If It Breaks | Accepting Owner; may say “None”
Entry CriteriaYesChecklist
Exit CriteriaYesChecklist, objectively checkable
Execution OrderYesNumbered, risk-first
Time Budget vs EstimateYesPer tier + total

Tiers mean: Tier 1 must run, Tier 2 should run, Tier 3 optional. Every Tier 1 row must name its justification, and every exclusion must name an owner who accepts the risk.

6

Run the self-check before you trust the output

Six checks. The first three decide whether the scope is honest; the budget check decides whether it is achievable.

  • Every changed area appears in Tier 1.
  • All business-critical paths are represented regardless of change status.
  • Hotspot areas are included, or their exclusion is justified and risk-accepted.
  • Time estimate totals against the budget, with overrun flagged.
  • Exit criteria are objectively checkable.
  • No invented historical defect data.
Fastest sanity check. Add up the Time column and compare it to the budget yourself. If the numbers were never added, the plan is a list of intentions.
7

Fix the failure, not the whole prompt

SymptomRule breachedCorrection to send
An unchanged area is assumed safe.No safety by omission.“Either bring it into Tier 1/2 or record it as an accepted residual risk.”
The suite exceeds the budget with no comment.Flag the overrun.“State the overrun and propose what to cut or what extra time is needed.”
A Tier 1 row has no justification.Every Tier 1 inclusion justified.“Cite change impact, criticality, or defect history.”
An exclusion has no accepting owner.Named risk acceptor.“Add the accepting owner or move the area into scope.”
Hotspot data looks invented.No invented history.“Replace with Not supplied. and adjust the rationale.”
Exit criteria are missing or vague.Objectively checkable.“Add exit criteria a reviewer could verify without asking you.”

9

Turn it into a skill

Create skills/regression-testing/SKILL.md, paste the frontmatter below, use the template's prompt block as the body, then add the output contract and self-check verbatim.

---
name: regression-testing
description: Build a risk-based, time-budgeted regression suite with tiers, inclusion justification, excluded-area residual risk, entry and exit criteria, and a risk-first execution order. Use when planning regression for a release or sprint.
---

Trigger phrases: “plan regression testing”, “regression suite for this release”, “what should we re-test”, “regression scope”.

Procedure: gather change + criticality + history → tier candidates → select within budget → justify → exclusions with risk → entry/exit → order → budget check → self-check.
One framework per file. Do not merge this with another framework's skeleton. Blended sections make the contract ambiguous and break the SKILL.md conversion.
