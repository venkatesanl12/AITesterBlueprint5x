FrameworkSTAR
TaskStakeholder QA status and release verdict
Guide13 of 17

What it does. Reports testing progress to stakeholders with numbers: progress against plan, execution and defect metrics, trend, risks, a clear quality verdict, and the decisions the audience must make.

Use when

Weekly reporting, a milestone, or a release gate — anywhere someone senior needs to make a decision from your testing status.

Skip when

The audience is your own team at stand-up — use 10-daily-scrum-notes-STAR.md. Task-level detail in a status report buries the decision.

1

Read the framework before you paste anything

STAR gives the report a spine: the Situation bounds the numbers, the Task sets the honesty requirement, and the Result fixes the eight sections.

S
SituationThe release, change, budget and constraints you supply.
T
TaskThe one outcome required, stated in a sentence.
A
ActionThe ordered analysis steps the model must perform.
R
ResultThe numbered deliverable sections and their shape.
Rule of thumb. STAR is a planning and reporting frame, so the numbers live in S and the contract lives in R. Only S — Situation changes between runs.
2

Prepare the inputs first

Every percentage in the output must be derivable from the counts you supply. If you hand over adjectives, you get adjectives back.

  • Period, audience, release — who is reading decides the depth.
  • Plan vs actual — whether a plan was supplied determines if variance is possible.
  • Raw metrics — executed, passed, failed, blocked, not run; defects opened and closed.
  • Risk list — with owners, so mitigation is named rather than implied.
  • Prior report — so trend is a comparison rather than a claim.
3

PlaceholderWhat to writeExample

Most common mistake. Supplying percentages instead of counts. The template recomputes rates from counts — if you only supply a percentage, the arithmetic cannot be checked and the report inherits your error.
4

Audit the output against the contract

Eight sections. The executive summary is exactly three sentences, and the verdict must be derivable from the metrics above it.

SectionRequiredShape
Executive SummaryYesExactly 3 sentences: status, headline number, key risk
Progress Against PlanYesPlanned | Actual | Variance
Execution MetricsYesCounts + computed pass rate
Defect MetricsYesOpened / closed / open by severity + ageing count
Trend vs Prior ReportYesImproving / flat / worsening with numbers
Risks and IssuesYesRisk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner
Quality VerdictYesOn Track / At Risk / Off Track + reason
Decisions NeededYesDecision | Owner | Needed By; may say “None”

Pass rate denominator is executed cases, not planned cases — and the report must say so. Release blockers belong in the executive summary, not buried in risks.

5

What a good run looks like

Filled excerpt from the template — the verdict follows from the blocked count, and the blocking gap is in the summary rather than buried.

MetricValue
Executive SummaryTesting is At Risk. 42 of 60 planned cases executed (70%), pass rate 81%. One release-blocking gap remains: no locked-account test data, so 8 cases cannot run.
ProgressPlanned 60 | Executed 42 | Variance −18
ExecutionPassed 34 | Failed 6 | Blocked 8 | Not Run 12 | Pass rate 81% (34/42)
Quality VerdictAt Risk — 8 blocked cases depend on GAP-07 and cannot execute before the release date.

The denominator is stated openly: 34 of 42 executed, not 34 of 60 planned. Both numbers are true; only one is useful.

6

Run the self-check before you trust the output

Seven checks. Arithmetic first — a status report with wrong percentages damages trust faster than bad news does.

  • All arithmetic re-checked against supplied inputs.
  • Pass rate denominator is executed cases, not planned cases — and stated.
  • No qualitative hedging replacing a number.
  • Release-blocking risks appear in the executive summary.
  • Verdict is consistent with the metrics shown.
  • Trend compares like-for-like periods.
  • Every decision has an owner and a date.
Fastest sanity check. Recompute the pass rate from the table before sending. If the report says 81% and your arithmetic says 85%, the numbers came from different runs.
7

Fix the failure, not the whole prompt

SymptomRule breachedCorrection to send
A percentage cannot be derived from the counts.Derived, not asserted.“Remove the percentage or supply the counts behind it.”
“Almost done” appears with no number.No hedging.“Replace with remaining case count and days.”
Pass rate uses planned cases as the denominator.Executed denominator.“Recompute as 34/42 and state the denominator.”
A release blocker sits at the bottom of the risks.Surfaced in summary.“Move it into the executive summary.”
The verdict contradicts the metrics.Verdict derives from metrics.“Re-derive the verdict from the counts shown.”
A decision has no owner or date.Owner and date required.“Add both, or drop the row.”

9

Turn it into a skill

Create skills/qa-status-report/SKILL.md, paste the frontmatter below, use the template's prompt block as the body, then add the output contract and self-check verbatim.

---
name: qa-status-report
description: Produce a stakeholder QA status report with plan variance, execution and defect metrics, trend, risks, a clear quality verdict, and the decisions needed. Use for weekly, milestone, or release-gate reporting.
---

Trigger phrases: “QA status report”, “weekly test report”, “report testing progress”, “release readiness report”.

Procedure: gather metrics → progress vs plan → execution → defects → trend → risks → verdict → decisions → arithmetic check → self-check.
One framework per file. Do not merge this with another framework's skeleton. Blended sections make the contract ambiguous and break the SKILL.md conversion.
